Information Systems (IS) in the Digital Age

Week 01 · Management of Information Systems (IS) Assessment · Cyber.SoHo Educational Hub

This course is called Assessment, not Introduction, because it asks you to judge systems and defend the judgement — not merely describe them. Every claim you make must be falsifiable: stated precisely enough that evidence could show it wrong.

Student A — Opinion "The booking system is quite good. It is easy to use and most students seem to like it."
Student B — Assessment "Room occupancy rose from 41% to 68% over eight weeks. The claim that students prefer it has never been measured — that claim should be removed until it is."

Learning Outcomes

  1. State what an IS is via its four activities and three dimensions — without using "computer" as the definition.
  2. Distinguish data, information and knowledge with your own example.
  3. Name the six strategic business objectives and match a real system to at least two.
  4. Identify the four system types by management level (TPS/MIS/DSS/ESS).
  5. Describe a business process crossing departments, and why crossings lose information.
  6. Explain what an enterprise application is and what problem it solves.
  7. Apply the five competitive forces to a small business.
  8. List the five moral dimensions of IS and apply the five-step ethical analysis.
  9. Conduct a six-lens assessment defended by named measurements.
  10. Recognise the difference between an assessment and an opinion.

1.1 — Information Systems in Global Business Today

Data, Information, Knowledge

Data Raw recorded facts, no interpretation. 2026-09-14, JOB-1003, 2.75, 64.00
Information Data organised to answer a question actually asked: "Brake overhauls averaged 2.75h vs a 2.0h estimate."
Knowledge Understanding of why, usually living in a person, not a database: "...because most bikes now have disc brakes."

The Four Activities of an Information System

ActivityWhat happensFailure mode when weak
InputRaw facts captured from inside/outside the org.Wrong/missing data poisons everything downstream.
ProcessingFacts sorted, calculated, validated, combined.Out-of-date rule: arithmetic right, answer wrong.
OutputResult reaches a person who can act on it.Correct but too late, wrong format, wrong person.
FeedbackResult examined, changes the input next time.Same flawed report runs for years, unread.

Feedback is the activity most beginners leave out — it's what turns data-processing into a genuine information system.

Six Strategic Business Objectives

  • 1. Operational excellence — less waste, time, errors.
  • 2. New products/business models — sell something new, or newly.
  • 3. Customer/supplier intimacy — know them individually.
  • 4. Improved decision making — measurement in time to act.
  • 5. Competitive advantage — hard for rivals to copy.
  • 6. Survival — law/regulator leaves no alternative.

Claiming two objectives precisely scores higher than claiming six vaguely.

Illustrative Objective Mix

Complementary Assets

Two firms buy the same software; one's productivity rises, the other's doesn't. Complementary assets — organisational, managerial, social — explain the gap. The software is rarely the expensive part.

Organisational

Supportive culture, efficient process, decentralised authority.

Managerial

Senior backing, incentives, training, knowledge sharing.

Social

Infrastructure, skilled labour market, standards, enforceable law.

Four System Types, Four Kinds of Question

TypeUsed byQuestion it answers
TPSFront-line staff/machines"Record this sale, shift, shipment."
MISMiddle managers"How did we do last month vs. plan?"
DSSAnalysts / specialists"What happens if we change this?"
ESSSenior management"Where is this organisation going?"

Data flows upward: bad data in the TPS becomes confident, well-formatted, wrong answers everywhere above it.

▸ Real-Life Example 1 — Atwater Bike Repair

IPO + Feedback

A two-mechanic Montréal shop runs one day: five jobs captured as input, labour/parts/tax as processing, a job sheet as output, and a booking-rule change as feedback (JOB-1005 finishes at 18:30, past the 17:00 close).

LABOUR_RATE = 62.00 ; TAX = 0.14975
stage_1_input()      -> capture 5 RepairJob records (no calculation)
stage_2_processing()  -> labour = hours*rate; total = (labour+parts)*(1+TAX)
                         finished = accepted_at + hours; overran = finished.hour >= 17
stage_3_output()      -> print job sheet, flag overruns with *
stage_4_feedback()    -> if late_jobs: "stop accepting >3h jobs after 11:00"
                         top_earner -> "feature this service on the window sign"

1.2 — Global E-business and Collaboration

A Business Is a Set of Business Processes

A customer buying coffee triggers order-to-cash across four functions. The crossings between departments are where information gets lost, delayed, duplicated or contradicted.

1. Sales — takes order 2. Operations — prepares item 3. Finance — collects, taxes, records 4. Supply — deducts stock, reorders

Other cross-cutting processes: procure-to-pay, hire-to-retire, record-to-report.

Four Enterprise Applications (the walls-crossers)

ERP Enterprise Resource Planning

One database covering finance, HR, manufacturing, sales, distribution at once. Removes walls — and departmental independence.

SCM Supply Chain Mgmt

Coordinates suppliers/production/distribution beyond the firm's own walls.

CRM Customer Relationship Mgmt

Every customer interaction survives the salesperson who leaves.

KMS Knowledge Mgmt System

Hardest to succeed — depends on busy people writing down what they know.

1.3 — Information Systems, Organizations, and Strategy

Two Economic Explanations for Flatter Firms

Transaction cost theory

IS lowers the cost of buying from the market, so firms outsource more and stay smaller.

Agency theory

IS lowers supervision (agency) costs, so one manager oversees more people — fewer middle layers.

Porter's Five Competitive Forces — Boba Blvd vs. Pearl & Co.

  • Rivalry → loyalty history that isn't price-comparable
  • New entrants → switching costs (stored value, saved preferences)
  • Substitutes → speed from demand-shift to launch
  • Buyer power → CRM segmentation, personalised offers
  • Supplier power → multi-source quoting portals

Total pressure: Boba Blvd 21/25 vs Pearl & Co. 12/25. Note the identical "substitute products" score — an honest assessment names what the app did not fix.

Four Generic Strategies

Low-cost leadership — inventory/process automation
Product differentiation — mass customisation
Focus on a niche — fine-grained customer analysis
Customer/supplier intimacy — CRM, extranets

Buying a system is not a strategy — every competitor can buy the same one. Advantage comes from the harder-to-copy complementary assets (§1.1.5).

Network Economics, Ecosystems, Disruption

Network economics

Each new participant makes the network more valuable to everyone already in it.

Business ecosystems

Niche firms build on a keystone firm's platform — a risk not on any balance sheet.

Disruptive technology

Arrives worse, improves, displaces — each ignoring decision was individually rational.

1.4 — Ethical and Social Issues in Information Systems

The StudySpot case: a campus room-booking app records student number, room, time slot, and the device's MAC address — data the booking function does not actually require.

Five Moral Dimensions of Information Systems

1. Information rights and obligations

What may be collected about a person, and what must be told to them.

2. Property rights and obligations

Who owns the data, the derived insights, and the software itself.

3. Accountability and control

Who can be named as responsible when the system causes harm.

4. System quality

What standard of data and software quality is acceptable, and who decides.

5. Quality of life

What values and institutions a system preserves or erodes.

Responsibility, Accountability, Liability

Responsibility

Accepting the consequences of a choice.

Accountability

A mechanism (e.g. a log) that records who did what — a property of a system, not a feeling.

Liability

Law permitting an affected person to seek a remedy.

Kant's test vs. the Utilitarian test

Kant's test asks whether it would be acceptable if every college did this — most conclude it would not. The Utilitarian test can go either way, since better occupancy data benefits many while harming none visibly. Naming both tests — and where they disagree — is the assessable move.

1.5 — The Six-Lens Assessment Method

Every graded assessment in this course answers six questions, in this order, each defended with one piece of evidence. Score the weakest lens, not the average — a system with five excellent dimensions and one failing dimension is a failing system.

LensThe questionWhere it comes from
1. PurposeWhich business objectives does it serve?Lesson 1.1.4
2. PeopleWho inputs, decides, is affected?Lessons 1.1.3 / 1.2.7
3. ProcessWhich steps does it change, remove, create?Lesson 1.2.1
4. StrategyWhich competitive force does it weaken?Lesson 1.3.5
5. Ethics (×2 weight)Whose data, whose risk, whose accountability?Lesson 1.4.2
6. Evidence (×2 weight)What measurement proves each claim?Lesson 1.0

Three Marking Rules

  1. A lens you skip is a mark you lose — all six, every time.
  2. Ethics and Evidence carry double weight.
  3. A score with no written finding scores zero — a number without a sentence is not an assessment.
