1. There is only one piece of work

The Course Evaluation Table shows two entries that both say 30%: "Continuous Course Assessments" and "Written Project." They are the same 30% — not 60%. One body of work, marked in three instalments as you build it. You get corrective feedback twice before the version that counts.

30% Broken Into Three Tasks

Task 01 + Task 02 + Task 03 = 30% of final grade (not 60%).

2. The rule that makes it a capstone

One subject. Three passes.

You choose one organisation and one system in Task 01, and keep both until 28 August. Tasks 02 and 03 go deeper into the same subject. You cannot switch.

≥3 roles At least three roles involved in the process.
One cycle A complete cycle you can observe in under 30 minutes.
Something to count A measurable element that carries you through all three tasks.

A subject chosen because it sounds impressive will not carry you through — choose carefully in Task 01.

4. What Each Pass Does

Parts B and C are described here so you can see where you are heading. The binding briefs arrive with Lectures 02 and 03 and may differ in detail — Part A will not change.

Part A — DESCRIBE (Task 01, this week)

Establish what the system is: its four activities, its three dimensions, one business process mapped across departments, a competitive-forces scorecard with evidence beside every score, and an ethics review. Ends with your six-lens baseline, an evidence log, and the questions you could not answer.

This is the foundation. Everything later either builds on it or corrects it.

Part B — DIAGNOSE (Task 02, provisional shape)

Go beneath the surface of the same system: its data, its integration with other systems, where information is duplicated or lost, and what the handoffs actually cost. You will close some of the open questions you wrote in A7.

Part C — JUDGE (Task 03, provisional shape)

Reach a defensible position. Recommend a change, cost it in something other than money, name what would have to be true for you to be wrong, and say how you would measure whether the change worked.

5. What the Final Dossier Looks Like

One PDF, roughly 5,000–6,500 words of prose plus tables and figures.

COVER — name · student number · subject organisation and system
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — one page, written last · ~350 words · strongest finding · weakest lens
CONTENTS — Part A (revised) · Part B (revised) · Part C (revised)
REFLECTION — ~400 words · what you got wrong in Part A and how you know · what you'd do differently
APPENDICES — consolidated evidence log · all figures · all scorecards · full source list

Two things exist only in the final version: the executive summary (written last, read first) and the reflection — a genuine account of what you got wrong, not a "thank-you note." Honest reflections score well.

6. How the Marks Work

Each task is marked out of 100 and scaled to 10%. The final dossier is marked on consolidation: whether you acted on feedback, whether the three parts read as one document, and whether the executive summary and reflection do their jobs.

The Six Lenses (weight)

1. Purpose — which business objectives does the system serve?×1
2. People — who inputs, who decides, who is affected?×1
3. Process — which steps does it change, remove or create?×1
4. Strategy — which competitive force does it weaken?×1
5. Ethics — whose data, whose risk, whose accountability?×2
6. Evidence — what measurement proves each claim is true?×2

The four rules that never change

1. Skip a lens, lose the markAll six, every time.
2. Double weightEthics and Evidence carry double weight.
3. No finding, no scoreA score with no written finding scores zero — not reduced, zero.
4. Report the weakest lensNot the average — averaging hides the thing you were asked to find.
