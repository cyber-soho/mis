1. The Brief, In One Sentence

Take the system you described in Task 01, look underneath it at four specific layers — infrastructure, data, network, security — and report where information is duplicated, where it is lost, and what the handoffs actually cost, with a measurement beside every claim.

0.3 Vocabulary

You are computer science students, but you are new to IS (Information System) assessment. Every term below appears in this task.

IT (Information Technology)The equipment: hardware, software, networks

System of recordThe one place where a fact is supposed to live authoritatively
HandoffA point where work passes from one person, team or system to another. Handoffs are where time and information are lost

APIAn agreed way for one program to ask another program for something
ETL (Extract, Transform, Load)Pulling data out of one system, making it consistent, loading it into another
DBMSThe software that stores data and refuses data that breaks its rules

SQLThe language used to ask a database questions
TCO (Total Cost of Ownership)Every cost over the life of a decision, not the purchase price

Block 1 — Concept (60 minutes)

Four diagnostic passes, fifteen minutes each. Each pass produces one written finding of roughly 40 words plus evidence. A pass with a score and no written finding scores zero.

B1 · The Infrastructure Pass (15 min)

IT Infrastructure and Emerging Technologies

Question: what does your system run on, who is responsible for which layer, and what does that arrangement cost over five years?

  1. Decide the rental model: on-premises, IaaS, PaaS or SaaS.
  2. Draw the responsibility split for each layer (application, runtime, OS, virtualization, servers/storage, networking, and your organisation's data) as "organisation" or "provider".
  3. Answer: which layer, if it failed, would the organisation still be accountable for even though the provider operates it? Usually the data row.
  4. Build a five-year TCO sketch: current arrangement vs one plausible alternative.
  5. Include staff time — hours/week × weeks/year × hourly rate. Most-often-omitted, usually the largest variable.

Finding (~40 words): state the model, name the layer where responsibility and accountability diverge, and give the five-year figure with the labour rate you assumed.

B2 · The Data Pass (15 min)

Foundations of Business Intelligence: Databases and Information Management

Question: where does the data live, where is the same fact stored twice, and what question can this system not answer?

  1. List the entities your system handles (3–6 is normal).
  2. For each entity name the system of record.
  3. Hunt for duplication: is any fact typed in twice (main system + spreadsheet/email/form/second system)?
  4. Name which of the five diseases of a file environment is present: redundancy, inconsistency, program–data dependence, poor security, or lack of data sharing.
  5. Name one question a manager would reasonably ask that the system cannot answer, and why (missing key/field/link).

Finding (~40 words): one duplicated fact, its consequence named in Week 02 vocabulary, and one unanswerable management question with the structural reason.

B3 · The Network Pass (15 min)

Telecommunications, the Internet, and Wireless Technology

Question: how does a request travel through this system, and where does the delay or loss actually sit?

  1. Trace one transaction end to end: user device → access network → internet → provider → back (6–9 steps).
  2. Mark every step where work passes between people or systems — the handoffs.
  3. For the slowest/most fragile handoff, estimate bandwidth (Mbps) and latency (ms) separately; say which one the complaint is actually about.
  4. If wireless is involved, ask whether airtime contention explains the symptom before assuming a capacity shortage.
  5. Cost the handoff in minutes, re-keying steps, or information lost in transit.

Finding (~40 words): name the constraint, distinguish it from the constraint people assume, and give the handoff cost in your chosen unit.

B4 · The Security Pass (15 min)

Securing Information Systems

Question: what would go wrong, how much would it cost per year, and is the obvious fix worth buying?

  1. Name one realistic incident: phishing/social-engineering, credential reuse, unpatched component, or insider error.
  2. Classify damage against the CIA triad: breached or not affected, with one clause of justification each.
  3. Identify which of the seven defence-in-depth layers would have to fail (physical, perimeter, network, endpoint, application, data, or people-and-process). Layer 7 is the answer far more often than expected.
  4. Quantify SLE and ARO, multiply for ALE.
  5. Propose two controls (one costs money, one costs only a process change) and compute ROSI for both: (ALE_before − ALE_after − Cost) / Cost. Report both, including if one is negative.

Finding (~40 words): the incident, the failed layer, the ALE, and which of your two controls the arithmetic actually supports.

Ethical boundary: you are describing a plausible incident from published sources and ordinary observation — not probing, scanning, testing, or reporting a real vulnerability. If you believe you've accidentally discovered a genuine weakness, do not write it here — contact your instructor privately.

Producing Part B.

Pull B2 and B3 together. State, in connected prose: one place information is duplicated (and what disagreement that permits); one place information is lost (a field on one side of a handoff, absent on the other); what the handoffs cost, in minutes, re-keying steps, or lost fields.
