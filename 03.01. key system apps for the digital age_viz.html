Lesson 1 — Achieving Operational Excellence & Customer Intimacy

Two goals: operational excellence (faster, cheaper, fewer errors) and customer intimacy (knowing customers individually). Both are information problems, solved by enterprise applications built around one shared database.

Silos vs. ERP

  • Information SiloEach department keeps a private copy of data. The copies drift apart — nobody can prove which number is true.
  • ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)One integrated system, one shared central database. A sale updates inventory, revenue and loyalty points instantly — a single source of truth.

🌍 Real-life example — "Poutine Palace" grows up

Léa's five-location chain suffered stock-outs and a "stranger" customer experience under silos. After installing a small cloud ERP + CRM module, one sale updates stock, revenue and Amira's loyalty points everywhere at once — operational excellence and customer intimacy from the same design decision: one shared database.

🔗 SCM — beyond your own walls

The bullwhip effect: small demand changes amplify into large order swings up the supply chain when data isn't shared. SCM systems move companies from a push model (forecast-driven) to a pull model (sale-driven).

🤝 CRM — the customer-intimacy machine

Gathers every customer interaction into one view. Tracks churn rate (% of customers lost) and CLTV (expected lifetime profit per customer).

⚠️ The honest cost: Enterprise applications are expensive, force companies to change work habits, and create vendor switching costs. Installing the software is the easy half — changing how people work is the hard half.

Lesson 3 — Managing Knowledge

Vocabulary discipline first: Data (raw facts) → Information (organized, meaningful) → Knowledge (information + experience, guides action) → optionally Wisdom (DIKW hierarchy).

Tacit Knowledge Lives in a person's head and hands. Learned by experience, hard to write down — walks out the door when the person leaves.
Explicit Knowledge Written down in manuals, FAQs, checklists. Stored, searched, copied, taught. Survives the departure of its author.

The knowledge management value chain

1️⃣
Acquire

Applying knowledge reveals gaps → feeds back into Acquire. The cycle is a circle, not a line.

🌍 Real-life example — the help desk and the retiring technician

Marc retires after 22 years. Supervisor Priya captures his fixes as searchable articles before he leaves. When the Monday printer and the H-214 projector fail again, a first-week employee solves both in minutes — "Marc left; what Marc knew stayed."

🤖 This course's one rule about AI: "The model drafts. The analyst decides. The analyst's name goes on the record."

Lesson 4 — Enhancing Decision Making

StructuredClear, repeatable rule (30-day refund policy). Served by MIS / TPS.
Semi-structuredPart calculable, part judgment (café evening hours). Served by DSS.
UnstructuredNovel, no formula (expand to Toronto?). Served by ESS.

Herbert Simon's four decision stages

  1. Intelligence — discover the problem
  2. Design — model the options
  3. Choice — pick one, using the numbers
  4. Implementation — act, then check, and loop back
